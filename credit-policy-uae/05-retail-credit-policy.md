## 1. Purpose, scope and principles

### 1.1 Purpose
This policy sets the rules under which the Bank provides Sharia-compliant finance to individual customers in the UAE. It aims to grow the retail book safely, comply fully with the Central Bank of the UAE (CBUAE), and offer terms competitive with the leading UAE retail banks.

### 1.2 Scope
- **Customers:** UAE nationals and UAE-resident expatriates, salaried, self-employed and retired. Non-residents are out of scope for this version.
- **Products:**
  - **Unsecured:** personal finance without salary transfer; Islamic credit cards.
  - **Salary-assigned:** personal finance secured by the employer's salary transfer confirmation and end-of-service undertaking, including buyout and top-up.
  - **Secured:** home finance, auto finance, deposit-backed, gold-backed and end-of-service-benefit (EOSB)-backed finance; salary advance.

### 1.3 Principles
1. **Regulation first.** CBUAE rules are minimum standards. The Bank may be stricter and is never more lenient.
2. **Sharia compliance.** Every product uses a structure approved by the Internal Sharia Supervisory Committee, in line with the Higher Sharia Authority standards.
3. **Responsible finance.** Affordability is assessed for every customer. No customer is financed beyond what they can reasonably repay.
4. **Competitive but safe.** Terms sit at or slightly better than the market median for customers, with stronger risk controls.
5. **Fair treatment.** No customer is excluded on nationality, gender or religion. Decisions rest on verified income, conduct and risk.
6. **AI recommends; people decide.** The AI Risk Score Card provides grades, recommendations and explanations. Credit staff decide and are accountable (Section 8).

## 2. Regulatory and Sharia framework

| Source | Key requirements applied in this policy |
|---|---|
| CBUAE Regulation 29/2011 (Bank Loans and Other Services Offered to Individual Customers), as amended | Personal finance ≤ 20× monthly income and ≤ 48 months; DBR ≤ 50% of gross salary and regular income (30% for retirees); auto finance ≤ 80% of value and ≤ 60 months; top-up only after 12 months of clean repayment; fee caps |
| CBUAE Mortgage Regulation (Circular 31/2013, as amended 2019, 2020, 2023) | Home finance LTV caps (85/80, 75/70, 65/60, off-plan 50%); 25 years; DBR 50% (60% for government housing programmes) |
| Board Resolution 96/By Circulation/2019 | Early settlement ≤ 1% or AED 10,000; processing fee caps |
| CBUAE Consumer Protection Regulation and Standards | Key Facts Statement, responsible financing, express consent for limit increases, complaints handling, cooling-off period (verify current days) |
| Al Etihad Credit Bureau (AECB) regulations | Credit report with prior written consent before every credit decision; monthly reporting |
| CBUAE Credit Risk Management Regulation C 3/2024 | Credit framework, independent credit review, IFRS 9 staging (> 30 DPD Stage 2; > 90 DPD Stage 3) |
| Sharia | Commodity Murabaha / Tawarruq, Murabaha, Ijarah Muntahia Bittamleek, Istisna'a with forward Ijarah, rahn (pledge). No compounding of late charges; late-payment amounts donated to charity; rebate of unearned profit (Ibra) on early settlement |

The CBUAE removed the AED 5,000 minimum salary for personal finance in November 2025. Minimum salaries in this policy are the Bank's own choice.

## 3. Definitions
- **Gross monthly income:** basic salary plus fixed allowances and regular income from a defined source, verified by salary certificate, Wage Protection System (WPS) credits, bank statements or UAEFTS consent. Variable pay is counted at the 12-month average after a 50% haircut. Self-declared income is never used alone.
- **DBR (debt burden ratio):** total monthly obligations to all lenders ÷ gross monthly income. Credit cards count at 5% of the limit. Overdraft-type facilities count at 5% of the limit.
- **Salary-assigned finance:** finance where the employer confirms salary transfer to the Bank on the Bank's format and gives an irrevocable undertaking to pay end-of-service benefits to the Bank.
- **Unsecured (non-STL) finance:** finance where the salary is paid to another bank and repayment is collected by UAE Direct Debit (UAEDDS).
- **Employer category:**
  - **A:** federal and local government, semi-government, Ministry of Defence, Armed Forces, Ministry of Interior.
  - **B:** listed large private employers (approved list).
  - **C:** other listed private employers.
  - **D:** non-listed employers.
  - Annex B gives the listing criteria.
- **AI grade:** the AI Risk Score Card risk grade, 1 (lowest risk) to 10 (highest), with a 12-month probability of default.
- **Deviation:** approval of a case that fails a Bank-policy rule (never a CBUAE rule), within Section 9.

## 5. General credit criteria (all products)

### 5.1 Eligibility
| Criterion | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Nationality | UAE nationals and UAE-resident expatriates. No nationality is excluded except under sanctions screening |
| Minimum age | **21 years** (all products) |
| Maximum age at end of finance | Personal finance, cards, auto: **65 nationals / 60 expats**. Home finance: **70 nationals and self-employed / 65 salaried expats**. Retirees: pension-based finance to 70 (nationals) |
| Minimum income | Per product (Section 6). Default **AED 5,000** |
| Length of service | Salary-assigned: **3 months**. Unsecured non-STL: **6 months** [A]. Home finance: **6 months and probation completed** [A] |
| Self-employed | Trade licence **≥ 2 years** (personal and auto finance); **≥ 3 years** with **2 years' audited financials** (home finance). **12 months' bank statements** in all cases |

### 5.2 Credit bureau (AECB) [P]
| Product / track | Minimum AECB score | Treatment below minimum |
|---|---|---|
| Salary-assigned personal finance, cards, home, auto | **541** (nationals 500) | Decline; refer if secured by deposit |
| Unsecured non-STL personal finance | **600** | Decline |
| Deposit-backed finance | none | Refer if < 541 |

**Hard knock-outs, all products:**
- current arrears ≥ 30 days on any facility
- any 90+ days past due, write-off or settlement for less than the full amount in the last 24 months
- court case for unpaid debt

More than 6 AECB enquiries in 30 days → refer.

### 5.3 Affordability (DBR)
- **DBR ≤ 50%** of gross monthly income after the new finance; **≤ 30%** for retirees (pension). Government housing programmes: up to **60%**.
- Home finance: DBR is assessed at the **higher of the initial profit rate and the reversion rate** (current 3-month EIBOR plus the reversion margin) [P].
- The Bank does not lend at the DBR maximum automatically. It considers essential expenses and dependants.
- The AI Risk Score Card also reports a **basic + housing** DBR as a risk indicator. It is not a policy gate.

### 5.4 Verification and documents
- **All customers:** Emirates ID, passport and residence visa (expats), AECB consent, signed application.
- **Salaried, salary-assigned:** salary certificate or salary transfer letter on the Bank's format addressed to the Bank; **3 months'** bank statements or UAEFTS consent; employer's EOS undertaking.
- **Salaried, non-STL:** salary certificate; **12 months'** bank statements showing regular salary credits; UAEDDS mandate.
- **Self-employed:** trade licence, memorandum of association, **12 months'** personal and company statements, **audited financials (2 years) for home finance** and where available for other products.
- Annex C lists documents by product.

### 5.5 Sector watch-list [P]
The Credit Risk function keeps a sector watch-list for employers under stress, such as hospitality, travel or real estate in a downturn. Watch-listed sectors get **lower maximum amounts and AI-grade caps**, not blanket exclusion. Changes follow maker-checker.

### 6.3 Buyout, top-up and rescheduling
- **Buyout** (taking over finance from another bank):
  - **Security:** salary transfer confirmation and EOS undertaking are required.
  - **Liability letter:** issued **no more than 15 days** before disbursement.
  - **Payment:** the Bank pays the other bank directly **within 30 days**, and releases any remaining funds to the customer only after the **clearance letter** is received.
  - **Limits:** the combined finance stays within 20× income and 48 months.
  - **Fee:** no processing fee on a pure buyout.
- **Top-up:**
  - **Eligibility:** only after **≥ 12 months of clean repayment** (CBUAE).
  - **Checks:** fresh DBR and AECB check.
  - **Frequency:** at most **one top-up per 12 months**.
  - **Fee:** processing fee on the increment only.
- **Rescheduling:**
  - **Hardship:** under the collections policy, within CBUAE rules.
  - **Beyond 48 months:** only when DBR exceeds 50% and with **no new money**.

## 7. Pricing and charges
1. Pricing is **risk-based** by AI grade within the product floor and ceiling:

| AI grade | Probability of default (12 months) | Personal finance add-on over floor |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | < 1% | + 0.00–1.00% |
| 4–6 | 1–5% | + 1.00–5.00% |
| 7–8 | 5–12% | + 5.00–10.00% (referral only) |
| 9–10 | ≥ 12% | Not offered |

2. All fees are within CBUAE caps and published in the tariff and in a **Key Facts Statement** given before contract, in Arabic and English, showing the **APR**.
3. Late-payment amounts are **donated to charity** net of actual costs and never treated as income. There is **no compounding**.
4. Early settlement includes a rebate of unearned profit (**Ibra**).
5. VAT applies at the prevailing rate.

## 8. Credit decisioning and the AI Risk Score Card
1. Each application is checked against **three layers** in order: CBUAE rules, this policy (Bank policy), and the credit parameters (Section 6).
2. **Policy always wins.** A failed knock-out → **Decline**. A failed referral rule → **Refer**. All rules passed → **Approve**, unless the AI grade is **7 or worse**, in which case the case is **referred** to a credit officer.
3. **The AI never approves a case that the policy rejects.**
4. The recommended amount is the **lowest** of all applicable caps: CBUAE, product cap, multiple of income, DBR headroom, collateral advance rate and the amount requested. The binding constraint is shown.
5. Each decision is recorded with the policy version, model version, inputs and reasons, and can be replayed exactly.
6. The AI Risk Score Card may draft explanations and credit memos. They are drafts until a credit officer signs them.

## 10. Security, documentation and disbursement
1. All security documents are executed and, where applicable, registered **before disbursement**: Murabaha contracts, Ijarah and mortgage registration, RTA vehicle mortgage, deposit lien, gold pledge, employer letters.
2. Takaful policies are assigned to the Bank before disbursement.
3. Customers receive the KFS and contract in Arabic and English. The cooling-off period follows the CBUAE Consumer Protection Standards (verify current days).
4. For buyouts, funds go **directly** to the other bank.

---

## 11. Monitoring, early warning and collections
1. The AI Risk Score Card **re-scores the whole book nightly**. Watch-list triggers include:
   - salary not credited
   - a drop in salary credits
   - card utilisation above 85%
   - new arrears elsewhere (AECB)
   - spending above income (lifestyle mismatch)
2. Early-warning cases are reviewed **within 5 working days**.
3. **Collections:** contact strategy, hardship assessment and rescheduling as permitted by CBUAE (no new money beyond 48 months). There are no compounding charges.
4. **Classification and provisioning** follow CBUAE C 3/2024 and IFRS 9: > 30 days past due → Stage 2; > 90 days past due → Stage 3 (retail sub-bands 91–120 / 121–180 / > 180 days).
5. **Write-off** follows the provisioning policy and CBUAE minimum provisions.

## 12. Consumer protection
- KFS before contract; APR disclosure; express consent for any limit increase; no automatic limit increases.
- Complaints acknowledged and resolved within the CBUAE timelines.
- Vulnerable customers (for example illness, job loss or retirement) receive tailored treatment and forbearance.
- Arabic and English communications.

## Annex E: Sharia structures
- **Commodity Murabaha / Tawarruq:** the Bank buys a commodity, sells it to the customer at cost plus profit on deferred terms; the customer sells it for cash. Used for personal finance, salary advance, cards and buyout.
- **Murabaha:** cost-plus sale of a vehicle or goods. Used for auto finance.
- **Ijarah Muntahia Bittamleek:** lease ending in ownership. Used for completed homes.
- **Istisna'a with forward Ijarah:** used for homes under construction.
- **Rahn:** pledge of deposits, gold or EOSB rights as security.
