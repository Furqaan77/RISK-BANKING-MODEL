# Benchmark & Gap Report: UAE Islamic Bank Retail Credit Policy v0.1

Prepared 2026-11-09 for the bank's management and credit team. It compares the bank's basic credit criteria with the published criteria of the **top five UAE retail banks** and their Islamic arms, rates each item, and recommends changes. Source of truth: `03-parameter-sheet.md`. Benchmark data: `01-benchmark-unsecured.md`, `02-benchmark-secured.md`.

**Verdict:** of the 54 policy items, **31 are near** the top five, **17 slightly off** and **6 far**. The basic criteria are close to the market on pricing, fees, DBR, finance-to-value, tenors and salary-assigned finance.

**The six far gaps:**

- **P-08**: AECB credit score floor is missing
- **P-21**: Unsecured (no salary transfer) minimum salary of AED 5,000 is the most lenient in the market
- **P-28**: Credit card limit rule is missing
- **P-31**: Home finance minimum salary of AED 5,000 is far below the market
- **P-52**: Gold-backed finance at 90% of value has no market precedent
- **P-53**: EOSB-backed finance at 90% of accrued EOSB has no market precedent

**Headline:** after the recommended changes, every item sits inside the top-five range.

## 5. How to close the six far gaps

### P-08: AECB credit score floor is missing

- **What the top five do:** Only RAKBANK publishes floors (541 for expats and no-STL; 300 for nationals with STL). ENBD uses the score to waive salary-transfer, listing and service requirements. The other banks apply the score internally but do not publish a number.
- **Risk if unchanged:** Without a floor, applicants with poor repayment records at other lenders can qualify on salary alone. Unsecured approvals would carry losses the top five avoid, and decisions would be inconsistent between officers.
- **Recommended change:** Adopt score floors by product: salary-assigned ≥ 541 (nationals ≥ 500); unsecured no-STL ≥ 600; cards, home and auto ≥ 541; deposit-backed no floor (refer below 541). Add knock-outs: current DPD ≥ 30; any 90+ DPD, write-off or settlement in 24 months. More than 6 AECB enquiries in 30 days goes to referral.
- **Trade-off:** A small share of thin- or poor-record applicants is declined or referred. In return, unsecured losses stay in line with peers. The AI model's grade still decides inside the floor, so good customers above it are not penalised.
- **Worked example:** On the ADCB band scale, 300-540 is Poor, 541-650 Fair, 651-710 Good, 711-745 Very good, 746-900 Excellent. The proposed floors keep Poor out of unsecured lending and send Fair applicants without salary transfer to the 600 floor.

### P-21: Unsecured (no salary transfer) minimum salary of AED 5,000 is the most lenient in the market

- **What the top five do:** The top five ask about AED 10,000 for no-STL or non-listed employers (FAB non-listed 10,000; ENBD no-STL 10,000 per comparison sites). FAB Islamic asks AED 15,000 plus 18 months' service. Only RAKBANK and Mashreq NEO lend at AED 5,000, and RAKBANK requires AECB ≥ 541 and caps expats at 10x salary / AED 200k.
- **Risk if unchanged:** No-STL finance has no salary assignment, so repayment depends on the customer keeping the direct debit funded. At AED 5,000 with no further controls the bank would be the easiest no-STL lender among peers and would attract applicants other banks decline.
- **Recommended change:** Keep AED 5,000 (the user's criterion) but only with compensating controls: AECB ≥ 600, employer Cat A-C, ≥ 6 months' service, 12 months' statements showing a regular salary, repayment by UAE direct debit. For Cat D (non-listed) employers or AECB 541-599, require AED 10,000 (the market median). Caps: nationals 20x salary max AED 500k; expats 10x max AED 200k.
- **Trade-off:** This keeps the bank competitive for good low-income customers with clean records and a stable employer, the inclusion goal. It removes the riskiest combination (low income, no salary transfer, weak score, unlisted employer).
- **Worked example:** A AED 5,000 salary at 50% DBR supports up to AED 2,500 a month. Over 48 months at 9.99% that is about AED 98,589, but the expat cap (10x salary) limits it to AED 50,000 and the national cap (20x) to AED 100,000.

### P-28: Credit card limit rule is missing

- **What the top five do:** No top-five bank publishes a limit multiple. Card limits count in the DBR (market practice: 5% of the limit as a monthly obligation).
- **Risk if unchanged:** Without a rule, limits depend on officer judgement, DBR headroom is consumed unevenly, and the AI model has no ceiling to test against.
- **Recommended change:** Limit = lower of 3x gross monthly income (salary-assigned) or 2x (no STL), the tier cap (entry / Gold / Platinum), and the DBR headroom with 5% of the limit counted as a monthly obligation.
- **Trade-off:** These multiples sit within typical UAE practice and leave room for growth reviews (6 and 12 months of good use).
- **Worked example:** A AED 5,000 salary: salary-assigned limit AED 15,000 (5% = AED 750 a month counted in DBR); no-STL limit AED 10,000 (AED 500 a month).

### P-31: Home finance minimum salary of AED 5,000 is far below the market

- **What the top five do:** Top-five minimums: nationals AED 8,000 (EI, ADCB) to 15,000 (ADIB without STL), median 10,000; expats AED 10,000 (DIB) to 20,000 (EI), median 15,000.
- **Risk if unchanged:** Home finance is a 25-year commitment that has to survive rate rises. At AED 5,000 the affordable finance is small, documentation and valuation costs are high relative to the loan, and the customer has little buffer after rent-free ownership costs (service charges, maintenance, takaful).
- **Recommended change:** AED 10,000 for nationals (AED 8,000 under government housing schemes such as SZHP, MBRHE and ADHA top-up) and AED 15,000 for expats, the top-five medians.
- **Trade-off:** Fewer low-income home finance applications. Those customers remain eligible for personal and auto finance, and nationals keep access through government schemes.
- **Worked example:** A AED 5,000 salary at 50% DBR supports AED 2,500 a month. Over 25 years at a ~6% stressed rate that funds about AED 388,017 (about AED 461,955 at the 4.24% teaser rate). At 80% finance-to-value the property would be about AED 485,021 (about AED 456,491 for a national at 85%). At AED 10,000 the stressed finance is about AED 776,034; at AED 15,000 about AED 1,164,051.

### P-52: Gold-backed finance at 90% of value has no market precedent

- **What the top five do:** Only Emirates Islamic lends against gold, at 50% of gold certificates and only for Priority customers. No other top-five bank offers it.
- **Risk if unchanged:** Gold prices can fall more than 10% within days (for example April 2013). At 90%, a 10% fall leaves no cover at all, and the bank must sell the collateral in a falling market.
- **Recommended change:** Maximum 70% of gold value; top-up call at 80% and sale at 85% finance-to-value; daily revaluation; tenor up to 24 months; allocated gold or gold certificates held by the bank; Sharia Board approval of the structure.
- **Trade-off:** A lower advance than the user wanted, but still more generous than the only market reference (50%). It gives the customer room to respond to a call before any sale.
- **Worked example:** At 70%, gold can fall 12.5% before the top-up call and 17.6% before a sale. At 90% there is no room for a call at all.

### P-53: EOSB-backed finance at 90% of accrued EOSB has no market precedent

- **What the top five do:** No top-five bank lends against end-of-service benefits on their own. DIB, ADCB, RAKBANK, Mashreq and ADIB (auto) take an assignment of EOSB as additional security alongside the salary. CBUAE allows personal finance to be repaid from salary and EOSB, within the 20x and 48-month limits.
- **Risk if unchanged:** EOSB is only paid when employment ends. It can be reduced by amounts the employee owes the employer, depends on the employer's ability to pay, and may sit in an approved savings fund under the voluntary alternative scheme. A 90% advance leaves no margin for these risks.
- **Recommended change:** Offer EOSB backing only inside salary-assigned finance, as additional security: finance against EOSB up to 80% of accrued EOSB; ≥ 3 years' service; irrevocable employer undertaking; EOSB recalculated every year; total finance still within 20x salary and 48 months.
- **Trade-off:** Slightly smaller advances than requested. In return it is the only structure that matches how the market already treats EOSB, and it gives long-service customers more without creating an untested product.
- **Worked example:** Basic salary AED 12,000 and 6 years' service: accrued EOSB = 21 days x 5 years + 30 days x 1 year = 135 days x AED 394.52 = about AED 53,260. At 90% the advance would be AED 47,934; at 80% it is AED 42,608.
