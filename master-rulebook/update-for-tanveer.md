## What Faisal bhai asked us to do
1. **Collect the central bank rules** for the **US, UK and UAE**:
   - retail banking credit policy
   - corporate banking credit policy
   - SME banking credit policy
   - finance houses (companies that lend cash or against gold)
2. **Stay inside the central bank rules.** If we go wider, the work gets too deep and confusing.
3. Treat the central bank rules as the **"master book"**. These are the hard limits: for example, age 18–65 and maximum loan amounts. Every bank must work inside them.
4. **Compare about four real banks in each country** against their own central bank's rules: UK banks against UK rules, UAE against UAE, US against US.
5. **Build a comparison tool** that shows where each bank stands.
6. Use it to advise banks like a consultant:
   - **"You are too rigid"**: you are missing business you could safely take.
   - **"You are taking too much risk"**: this sector or product is risky for you.
   - We "peel the onion", opening one layer at a time.
7. **Later, not now:** risk appetite and bad-loan levels. For example, on a 10 billion loan book, can you live with 2% bad loans when similar banks have 1%?
8. **After this:** sessions with legal, compliance and risk experts, then a business expert on how to reduce risk or lend more.

## Things to know
- **The UAE sets hard numbers;** for example, monthly loan payments can't exceed 50% of salary. The **US and UK mostly set principles**, such as "the lender must check the customer can afford it", and have very few fixed caps. So in the US and UK, most of a bank's limits are its own choice.
- **Corporate and SME:** banks do not publish their corporate or SME credit policies. We can only compare what their annual reports show, such as sector exposure and bad-loan ratios. This is a good topic for the expert sessions.
