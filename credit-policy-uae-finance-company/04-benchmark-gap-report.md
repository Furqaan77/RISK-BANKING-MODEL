# Benchmark and Gap Report: UAE Finance Company Retail Credit Policy

*9 November 2026. For management and the credit team. The PDF version has the charts; this file holds the same content with the chart data as tables.*

## 1. The answer in one page
- **What it is.** A retail credit policy for a pilot conventional finance company (FCR Part I licence, AED 150m capital). It keeps the bank-grade credit rules we recommended for UAE banks, and changes only what the finance-company rules or market require.
- **Where it stands.** Of 60 policy items, 38 sit inside the finance-company range, 3 are better for customers than the typical finance company, 4 are deliberately tighter, 5 have no finance-company peer, 8 are finance-company legal requirements, and 2 products are not offered in the pilot.
- **The one-line position.** Bank-grade customer selection, finance-company reach, and a price below the finance-company typical: employer-assigned loans from 12.99% against a typical 20–28%.
- **What limits growth is capital, not demand.** In years 0–2 liabilities may not exceed capital funds, so the whole loan book is capped at about AED 300m of assets. The policy caps the pilot at AED 250m of loans.
- **Before launch:** 26 items need management sign-off (20 [A] adjusted, 6 [P] proposals; list in section 7), and 10 regulatory points need Legal & Compliance to confirm them against the FCR rulebook.

## 4. How we stand out among finance companies
1. **Bank-grade selection with finance-company reach.** Published AECB floors (541 / 560 / 600), DBR that counts cards and BNPL, a rate-rise stress test and 12 months of statements for the self-employed. No UAE finance company publishes credit floors.
2. **A lower price for good risk.** Employer-assigned loans from 12.99% and non-assigned from 15.99%, against a finance-company typical of 20–28% and highs of 36–40%. Better selection pays for the lower price.
3. **Bank-style security without moving the salary.** The employer-assigned track (assignment letter, EOS undertaking and direct debit) gives the security of a bank salary-transfer loan to customers who keep their salary account where it is. Most finance companies take only a cheque.
4. **Small-ticket credit, done safely.** Salary advance from AED 3,000 net salary with a flat fee and at most 6 a year; BNPL (phase 2) capped below the legal limit. This serves workers banks do not lend to, without payday-loan pricing.
5. **Gold loans with a clear safety margin.** 70% loan-to-value (the only peer lends 75%) at 14.99%, with a published top-up call at 80% and sale at 85%. The customer has a 12.5% price cushion before any call.
6. **Explainable AI inside capital-aware limits.** Policy always wins over the AI grade; every decision can be replayed; limits watch leverage, single-borrower and portfolio concentration every night.

### Scenario 1: Mariam, staff nurse: a loan without moving her salary
| | |
|---|---|
| **Customer** | Expat staff nurse at a private hospital (employer category C). Gross salary AED 6,500; 8 months in the job; AECB score 585; one credit card with an AED 10,000 limit. |
| **Wants** | AED 60,000 over 48 months. She does not want to move her salary account. |
| **Banks** | Most banks need AED 8,000–10,000 without a salary transfer: decline. RAKBANK's no-transfer loan (AED 5,000, AECB 541, 12.99–22.49%) is the one bank route. |
| **Finance companies** | Finance companies approve at about 20–28% (Finance House from 20%; Deem 15.99–39.99%). |
| **Our policy** | Employer-assigned track: the hospital signs a salary-assignment letter and EOS undertaking; she repays by direct debit from her current account. AI grade 5 → **17.99%**. (The non-assigned track would need AED 7,000 salary at her score, so the assigned route is the right one.) |
| **Result** | Approved at AED 1,762 a month. She pays AED 24,585 interest instead of AED 33,893 at a typical 24%: a saving of **AED 9,308**. |

**The maths**

| Step | Working | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum loan | lower of 20 × 6,500 and AED 300,000 | AED 130,000 |
| Card counted in DBR | 5% × AED 10,000 limit | AED 500 |
| Monthly instalment | AED 60,000, 48 months, 17.99% reducing | AED 1,762 |
| DBR | (1,762 + 500) ÷ 6,500 | 34.8% (limit 50%) |
| Total interest | 48 instalments − AED 60,000 borrowed (exact, before rounding) | AED 24,585 |
| Same loan at 24% | typical finance company | AED 33,893 |

## 6. Gaps and improvements
### 6.1 Tighter than the market: keep or loosen?
| ID | Item | What the market does | Recommendation | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F-16 | AECB floor (non-assigned ≥ 560; ≥ 600 for the AED 5,000 entry) | No finance company publishes a floor; most lend to any customer in 'good standing' and price the risk. | Keep for the pilot. After 6 months of performance data, consider opening 541–559 on the non-assigned track for AI grades 1–4 only. | Lower volume in the near-prime segment; better loss rates against a sector NPL of 7.1%. |
| F-44 | Auto rate (new 3.49%, used 4.49% flat) | Aafaq from 2.5% flat; Al Futtaim 3% (dealer-subsidised captive); banks 1.75–3.19%. | Keep: lower prices lose money at finance-company funding costs. Seek dealer subsidies for 'from 2.99%' campaigns instead. | Auto stays a small, access-led product. |
| F-49 | Gold loan-to-value 70% (peer 75%) | Finance House lends 75%. | Keep 70% for the pilot; review after one gold-price cycle. | Slightly smaller loans; much lower risk of forced sales. |
| F-53 | BNPL caps (AED 10,000 / 1× income / 6 months) | Legal cap is AED 20,000 or 3 months' income, 12 months, fees ≤ 30%. Tamara lends up to AED 20,000. | Keep for phase 2 launch; raise towards the legal cap only with 12 months of clean data. | Smaller baskets; low arrears in a product with high fraud and over-borrowing risk. |

### 6.3 Deliberate gaps
- **Customers earning AED 3,000–4,999:** salary advance only, no personal loan. Payday-style loans at 36% (Finance House) serve this segment; revisit after 12 months of pilot data.
- **Home finance and deposit-backed loans:** not in the pilot (licence and FCR).
- **Price vs banks:** a customer who will move their salary gets a cheaper loan at a bank. The policy targets customers who will not or cannot.

### 6.4 Regulatory points for Legal & Compliance to confirm
1. FCR article numbers and values (capital, leverage, large exposures, liquidity, governance): read from search extracts because the rulebook returned HTTP 403.
2. Whether the short-term credit caps (AED 20,000 / 3 months' income / 12 months / fees ≤ 30%) bind a Part I finance company's salary advance and BNPL. The policy applies them anyway.
3. Whether employer salary-assignment letters and EOS undertakings in favour of a finance company are enforceable (no salary account is held).
4. Whether the November 2025 removal of the AED 5,000 minimum salary applies to finance companies and to cards.
5. The CBUAE maximum write-off period, and whether the Credit Risk Management Regulation C 3/2024 applies in full to finance companies.
6. Credit-card minimum income of AED 60,000 a year (Regulation 29/2011 Article 5).
7. The full Appendix 2 fee table (deferment AED 100, rescheduling AED 250, late-fee caps by product).
8. Disbursement within 10 business days, and the 5-business-day cooling-off waiver rules.
9. WPS-agent approval if the payroll-card route is used for salary advances.
10. No-objection letters needed for each phase 1 product line before launch.

## 8. Data notes and confidence
- **Bank figures are not market shares.** Bank retail-book sizes quoted elsewhere include group and overseas books. Only CBUAE totals (credit to individuals AED 645.8bn, Aug 2026; finance-company loans about AED 11.2bn, FSR 2025) are used for market-size comparisons and pies.
- **Finance-company status pie:** the 20 entities on the CB Register (Aug 2026), grouped by our reading of their websites and KFS documents. 'Active' means a published retail product and KFS; it is not a measure of size.
- **Loan-mix pie:** FSR 2025 shares of the whole finance-company sector, not of any one company.
- **Rates:** reducing balance unless marked flat. Flat rates are converted to reducing only where stated (Aafaq 8% flat is about 14.5%; our used-car 4.49% flat is about 8.3% reducing).
- **Ranges:** a bar shows the lowest and highest published value; a single dot means only one value (often a 'from' rate) is published.
- **Digital / BNPL lenders** (botim / CashNow, e& easyCredit, Tamara) are shown separately from full finance companies because they sell small, short loans.
- **Confidence:** H = read directly from a KFS, report or register; M = search extract (the FCR rulebook returned HTTP 403, so FCR values must be confirmed by Legal & Compliance). Finance companies publish no AECB floors, so the AECB comparison is qualitative.
- **Scenarios** use illustrative customers; every number is calculated by the build script from the policy values.
