## 1. What we are building

A **credit risk engine that runs entirely on a bank's own machines**. It scores **retail and
corporate borrowers** against three layers of policy: the **Central Bank of the UAE (CBUAE) rules**, the
**bank's own policy**, and the bank's **credit policy**. It then **ranks customers from most risky to
least risky**, gives each one an **Approve / Refer / Decline decision with reasons**, and recommends a
**maximum loan or card limit**. A machine-learning model trained on the bank's own history refines the
rule-based scorecard. A **local LLM** (no cloud) writes a plain-English credit note for each decision. One
codebase serves every bank. Each bank gets its own policy configuration, its own locally trained model,
and its own data folder.

## 2. Why: the business case

- A bank's loans are its **assets**. Customer deposits (CASA: current + savings accounts) are its **liabilities**. A
  typical example: ~AED 60 bn of deposits against ~AED 34 bn of loans. The gap is under-used funding that
  could be lent **if the risk can be measured and mitigated**.
- Credit policy is applied by hand (often 60–80 parameters). As a result it is inconsistent, and blanket
  rules **turn away good customers**. Examples: whole nationality groups declined, or long-tenure
  salary-transfer employees with large **end-of-service benefits (EOSB)** never offered credit, even
  though the salary transfer letter plus EOSB make them effectively secured.
- Banks hold behaviour data (salary credits, card spend, repayment history) that rarely feeds the credit
  decision.
- The pitch: *"Our model shows which customers are really risky and which you are avoiding without good
  reason. Lending more to the second group within the same risk appetite lifts net income."*

## 3. Hard constraints (non-negotiable)

1. **Data never leaves the bank.** No cloud APIs, telemetry or external calls at runtime. The LLM runs
   locally through Ollama on `localhost`. Disable Streamlit usage statistics (`gatherUsageStats = false`).
2. **One deployment per bank.** Configuration, trained models, outputs and audit logs live in
   `banks/<bank_id>/`. Nothing is shared across banks.
3. **Regulation first.** CBUAE rules are hard gates. A bank configuration may **tighten but never loosen**
   them. The policy loader must reject a looser bank config with a clear error.
4. **Explainable.** Every score, decision and limit must trace back to rule IDs, scorecard factors and model
   feature contributions.
5. **Works without history.** With no labelled default data, the rules plus the expert scorecard must work
   alone. The ML model switches on only when there is enough data and it passes validation.
6. **Auditable.** Every scoring run writes an immutable audit record: input file SHA-256, policy versions
   (file hashes), model version, output file hash, user and timestamp.
7. **Human in the loop.** Outputs are recommendations. The UI and reports must say so.

## 4. Scope of version 1

| Segment | Products | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Retail (individuals)** | Unsecured personal loan, credit card, salary-backed / EOSB-backed personal loan | Focus area: *Retail → Unsecured → AI risk assessment*. Salary transfer + EOSB modelled as quasi-security. |
| **Corporate / SME** | Term loan, working-capital line, overdraft | Five-dimension corporate framework (§6.2). |

**Out of scope for v1, but leave room in the schema:** mortgages, auto loans, broker-dealer / investment-bank

## 5. Policy architecture: three layers

```
 Layer 1  CBUAE regulation   (same for every UAE bank)    → config/regulators/cbuae.yaml
    │  bank may only tighten
 Layer 2  Bank policy        (segments, employer lists,    → banks/<id>/bank_policy.yaml
    │                         nationality groups, appetite)
 Layer 3  Credit policy      (product rules, scorecard,    → banks/<id>/credit_policy.yaml
                              grade cut-offs, limit matrix)
```

**Rule format.** Declarative YAML that a credit team can read and edit. Every rule has:
`id`, `layer`, `description`, `applies_to` (segment, product, nationality_group, employment_type),
`condition` (an expression over features), `severity` (`knockout` → Decline, `refer` → at most Refer,
`warn` → note only), `source` (document + section + URL), and `confidence` (`verified` / `unverified`).
Evaluate the expressions with a **small, safe expression evaluator** (an allow-listed AST walker). Never use
`eval`.

**Tighten-only validation.** For every CBUAE numeric limit (e.g. `dbr_max_pct`, `personal_loan_max_salary_multiple`,
`personal_loan_max_tenor_months`), the loader checks that the bank and credit policy values are no looser than CBUAE.
Each limit carries a direction (`max` or `min`) so the check is generic.

**Demo policy** (`banks/demo_bank/`, used only until the real policy is supplied). Build it from these
examples taken from the planning call:
- Retail products only for **salary-transfer** customers with a **salary transfer letter (STL)** signed
  by the employer.
- **Employer categories** with different caps: `government`, `semi_government`, `defence_moi_mod`,
  `blue_chip` (approved list), `private_other` (lowest caps or refer).
- **Nationality groups** `uae_national` / `expat` with different caps and ages. Example from the call:
  minimum age 18 vs 21 and maximum age at loan maturity 65 vs 60. These are bank policy: CBUAE sets no age
  limit for personal loans (Appendix A). The same applies to the **salary transfer requirement**, the **minimum
  salary**, and the **card-limit factor in DBR** (market convention 5%). All are bank policy, not regulation.
- **Restricted nationality list**: configurable. Default severity `refer`, not `knockout`.
- **EOSB-backed loan**: customers with ≥ 5 years' service may borrow up to a configured % of accrued EOSB
  with an employer undertaking. CBUAE explicitly allows personal loans to be repaid from "salary and end of
  service indemnity" (Reg 29/2011 Art 2(a)). The EOSB is computed with the UAE Labour Law gratuity formula
  (Appendix A), parameterised in config.
- Example bank caps per employer category, a card-limit matrix (salary multiple × grade), and the
  grade cut-offs in §7.4.

## 7. Scoring

### 7.1 Rule gates
Evaluate every applicable rule and return `{rule_id, layer, passed, severity, message, source}`. Any failed
`knockout` → Decline. The score is still computed for ranking and analysis. Any failed `refer` → at most Refer.

### 7.2 Expert scorecard (always on)
- **Retail:** points scorecard. Bin each factor (AECB band, DBR-after band, max DPD 12m, 90+ DPD 24m, years of
  service, employer category, salary-transfer stability, card utilisation, cash advances, spend volatility,
  bureau enquiries). Points come from `credit_policy.yaml`. Scale the total to **0–1000** (higher = safer).
- **Corporate:** each dimension scored 0–100 from its sub-factors, weighted into 0–1000.
- Map scorecard points to a PD (probability of default) with a configurable logistic calibration
  (`pd = 1/(1+exp((score − a)/b))`, defaults documented). Recalibrate it on labelled data when available.
- Document the default bins, points and rationale in `docs/scorecard_methodology.md`.

### 7.3 Machine-learning model (switches on when labels exist)
- **LightGBM** with **monotonic constraints** on features with a known direction, plus a **logistic-regression
  challenger** on WoE (weight-of-evidence) bins. Separate models for retail and corporate.
- Train **locally** on the bank's history. Use an out-of-time split (latest 20% by date) when dates exist, otherwise a stratified split.
  Calibrate probabilities (isotonic or Platt).
- **Excluded features (always):** nationality, nationality_group, gender, marital_status, religion, and age as a
  raw feature. Nationality affects outcomes only through explicit, visible policy rules.
- Explainability: per-customer contributions via LightGBM `pred_contrib=True` (TreeSHAP) mapped to
  human-readable reason codes. No separate `shap` dependency is needed.
- Every training run writes a **model card** (`banks/<id>/models/<version>/model_card.md` + JSON):
  data window, rows, default rate, AUC, Gini, KS, calibration table, PSI vs training data, top features, and
  champion vs challenger.
- **Activation gate:** ≥ 1,000 rows, ≥ 50 defaults (configurable), and out-of-time Gini ≥ 0.30. Otherwise the
  model is saved but stays inactive and the scorecard alone is used.
- Monitoring: PSI per run against the training population. Warn when PSI > 0.25.

### 7.4 Blend, grade, decision, ranking
- `PD_final = w·PD_ml + (1−w)·PD_scorecard`, with `w = 0.6` when an active validated model exists, else 0 (configurable).
- **Grades** (configurable PD bands): A < 1%, B 1–3%, C 3–7%, D 7–15%, E ≥ 15%.
- **Decision:** Decline if any knockout fails or the grade is E. Refer if any refer-rule fails, the grade is D, or PD is within
  a configurable margin of a grade boundary that changes the decision. Otherwise Approve.
- **Ranking:** sort by PD_final, highest first. Output the grade, decision, limit and top-3 risk drivers per row.
- **IFRS 9 stage hint** (informational): stage 3 if DPD ≥ 90 or the default flag is set; stage 2 if DPD ≥ 30 or there is a significant
  deterioration flag; else stage 1 (thresholds from Appendix A).

### 7.5 Limit recommendation
- **Retail personal loan:**
  `limit = min(CBUAE salary-multiple cap, bank cap for employer category × nationality group, product cap,
  DBR-headroom principal, requested_amount)`.
  DBR-headroom principal = remaining DBR allowance ÷ annuity factor(rate, tenor), with the tenor capped at the CBUAE max.
  EOSB-backed products add an EOSB-coverage cap.
- **Credit card:** card-limit matrix (salary multiple by grade), bounded by CBUAE and bank caps and by DBR headroom
  (using the card-obligation % from Appendix A).
- **Corporate:** `limit = min(stressed-DSCR capacity at the policy minimum DSCR, collateral-adjusted capacity
  (value × haircut by collateral type), large-exposure headroom (CBUAE % of capital − existing group exposure),
  policy cap, requested_amount)`.
- Always return the **binding constraint**, plus a "what would change the decision" hint (e.g. the max amount
  that passes DBR).

## 8. Explanations: local LLM

- Client for **Ollama** at `http://localhost:11434` (configurable). Temperature 0. JSON-structured prompt.
- **Pick the model from the detected hardware at install time** and write the choice to config:
  - GPU with ≥ 16 GB VRAM → a 14B-class instruct model (e.g. `qwen2.5:14b`)
  - GPU with 8–16 GB → an 8B-class model (e.g. `llama3.1:8b` or `qwen3:8b`)
  - CPU-only or < 8 GB → a 1.5–4B model (e.g. `qwen3:4b` / `qwen3:1.7b`) generated on demand only
  - No Ollama → template explanations only
- The LLM receives **only computed facts**: decision, grade, PD, failed rules, top drivers, binding limit
  constraint, and the what-if hint. **Guardrail:** after generation, every number in the text must appear in the facts.
  If a number does not, discard the text and use the template.
- Output: a 3–5 sentence credit-officer note, plus "what would change the decision".
- Scoring never depends on the LLM. For batch runs, generate narratives for the top-N riskiest customers and on
  demand in the customer view.

## 9. Local web dashboard (Streamlit, bound to `127.0.0.1` by default)

1. **Score customers:** choose bank and segment, upload a CSV/XLSX file, validate, run, and download the results (XLSX with
   Summary / Ranking / Rule results / Reasons sheets, plus CSV).
2. **Risk ranking:** filterable table (grade, decision, product, employer category), grade distribution, and
   decision mix.
3. **Customer detail:** score breakdown, rules passed/failed with sources, top drivers, limit waterfall
   showing the binding constraint, and the LLM note (generate button).
4. **Policy viewer:** the three layers side by side, sources, confidence flags, and tighten-only validation status. A demo-policy
   banner shows when relevant.
5. **Model lab:** upload labelled history, train, view the model card, activate or deactivate.
6. **Audit log:** past runs with hashes and versions.

No authentication in v1. Keep a clear hook for adding it later (e.g. reverse-proxy SSO).

## 10. Synthetic data

`python -m riskmodel.synth --segment retail --n 20000` and `--segment corporate --n 1000` generate
realistic UAE populations with a **known latent risk function**, so ranking quality can be measured:
- Retail: nationals and expats, employer categories, AED salaries (lognormal, by category), AECB-like scores
  300–900, DPD history, utilisation, spend patterns. ~3–6% default rate.
- Corporate: UAE sectors, financials consistent with each other, governance, collateral. ~4–8% default rate.
- A hand-crafted **golden set** (~20 rows per segment) of obvious best and worst cases, plus edge cases (age at
  maturity limit, DBR exactly at the cap, restricted nationality, missing optional fields).

## 12. Acceptance criteria

1. Fresh machine: `pip install -r requirements.txt` then `streamlit run riskmodel/app/Home.py` works. After
   installation, everything runs with networking disabled.
2. Scoring 20,000 retail rows (without LLM narratives) completes in under 60 s on a normal laptop CPU.
3. Synthetic data: ML AUC ≥ 0.75 retail and ≥ 0.70 corporate against the true labels. The scorecard alone ≥ 0.65.
   Golden-set worst cases land in grade D/E and best cases in A/B.
4. A bank config that loosens any CBUAE rule is rejected with a message naming the rule.
5. Every Decline lists at least one failed rule or driver with a source. Every limit names its binding constraint.
6. With Ollama stopped, everything works (template notes). The LLM guardrail rejects hallucinated numbers (unit test with a fake client).
7. Unit tests cover: the expression evaluator (including malicious input), tighten-only validation, DBR, EOSB and annuity
   maths, limit selection, scorecard scaling and PD mapping, the decision matrix, and the audit record.
