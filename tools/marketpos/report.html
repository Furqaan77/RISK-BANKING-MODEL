Where Our Credit Policy Stands
in the UAE Bank Market

We compared our draft retail credit policy with every UAE bank that publishes its retail lending terms: 33 banks, 16 measures, five products.

The short answer. Our credit policy is one of the easiest in the UAE to qualify for. It is easier than all 16 conventional banks with enough published data, and 5th of 22 banks on ease of access, behind only 4 Islamic banks. Its prices sit in the middle of the market, as intended. Overall it ranks 9th of 22, in the upper half of the market.

Three things to remember
  1. Easy to get. Entry salaries are at or below the market median on every product, and AED 5,000 without salary transfer (with safeguards) is the joint lowest in the UAE.
  2. Fairly priced. Rates sit near the market median. Only the home-loan fixed rate (4.24% vs a 3.99% median) is dearer than most banks.
  3. Different. Salary advance, end-of-service-backed finance and gold-backed lending are products the big conventional banks do not offer.

Part 5

How our policy stands out

Six things a customer, a broker or a regulator will notice.

1

We say “yes” where others say “no”, safely

  • No salary transfer: most top banks want AED 8,000–10,000. We accept AED 5,000 with AECB ≥ 600, a listed employer and 6 months in the job.
  • Car loans: most top banks want AED 7,000. We accept AED 5,000.
  • First home, UAE nationals: Mashreq and RAKBANK want AED 15,000. We accept AED 10,000, after a stress test.
2

Products the big conventional banks don’t offer

  • Salary advance before payday.
  • Finance secured by end-of-service benefits, priced near the floor.
  • Lending against gold, plus a clear published rule for card limits.
3

Fair, transparent pricing

  • Rates near the market middle, priced by risk grade, so good customers pay less.
  • No processing fee on buyouts.
  • Home loans: 25% free prepayment a year, lower valuation fee, processing capped at AED 50,000.
4

Speed

  • The AI Risk Score Card decides in about 3 seconds.
  • Buyouts paid to the other bank within 30 days.
  • UAE nationals get up to 120 days before the first instalment.
5

Safety built in

  • Credit-score floors and automatic declines for serious arrears.
  • Home loans stress-tested at a higher rate.
  • Nightly early warning on every customer; rate +2% if salary stops; portfolio limits.
6

Fair to everyone

  • No nationality bans.
  • Employers are grouped by risk, not excluded.
  • Every decision comes with clear reasons the customer can understand.

Part 6

Real customers, real answers

Eight realistic cases. The people are illustrative; market answers come from the banks' published terms (9 November 2026); the numbers use our policy's rates and rules.

Scenario 1

A nurse whose salary goes to another bank

Anita, 29, expat nurse at a listed private hospital (category C). Salary AED 6,500, paid to another bank. AECB 655, 14 months in the job, one card with an AED 10,000 limit.

What banks say
FAB (no salary transfer)Needs AED 10,000 → no
Emirates NBD (no salary transfer)Needs AED 10,000 → no
RAKBANK (no salary transfer)Yes: AECB ≥ 541; 10× salary at 12.99–22.49%
Mashreq NEOYes: up to AED 150,000 at 10.49–17.49%
Our policyYes. AECB 655 ≥ 600; listed employer; 14 months > 6
Our answer
  • Limit: lower of 10× salary and the AED 200,000 cap = AED 65,000.
  • Room each month: 50% of AED 6,500 = 3,250, minus AED 500 for the card = AED 2,750.
  • Instalment: grade 4 (11.99%), 48 months ≈ AED 1,711, inside the room.
Scenario 2

Moving a loan to us (buyout), UAE national

Khalid, 34, UAE national, Abu Dhabi government (category A). Salary AED 32,000. Existing loan: AED 400,000 left at 8.49%, 36 months to go.

What banks say
Other bank today8.49% on AED 400,000
Our policy5.49% (grades 1–3), paid to the other bank within 30 days, no processing fee
Our answer
  • Instalment falls from about AED 12,625 to AED 12,077: AED 549 a month, about AED 19,750 over 36 months.
  • After the other bank's 1% early-settlement charge (AED 4,000): net ≈ AED 15,750.
  • Top-up: +AED 100,000 → AED 500,000 (limit 20× = 640,000); over 48 months ≈ AED 11,626 = 36% of salary, under 50%.
