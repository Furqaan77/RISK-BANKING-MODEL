1. Purpose, scope and principles

1.1 Purpose

This policy sets the rules under which the Bank lends to individual customers in the UAE. It aims to grow the retail book safely, comply fully with the Central Bank of the UAE (CBUAE), and offer terms competitive with the leading conventional UAE retail banks.

1.2 Scope

  • Customers: UAE nationals and UAE-resident expatriates, salaried, self-employed and retired. Non-residents are out of scope for this version.
  • Products:
  • Unsecured: personal loans without salary transfer; credit cards.
  • Salary-assigned: personal loans secured by the employer's salary transfer confirmation and end-of-service undertaking, including buyout and top-up.
  • Secured: home loans (mortgages); auto loans; loans or overdrafts against fixed deposits; gold-backed and end-of-service-benefit (EOSB)-backed lending; salary advance.

1.3 Principles

  1. Regulation first. CBUAE rules are minimum standards. The Bank may be stricter and is never more lenient.
  2. Responsible lending. Affordability is assessed for every customer. No customer is lent more than they can reasonably repay.
  3. Competitive but safe. Terms sit at or near the market median for customers, with stronger risk controls.
  4. Fair treatment. No customer is excluded on nationality, gender or religion. Decisions rest on verified income, conduct and risk.
  5. AI recommends; people decide. The AI Risk Score Card provides grades, recommendations and explanations. Credit staff decide and are accountable (Section 8).

The CBUAE removed the AED 5,000 minimum salary for personal loans in November 2025. Minimum salaries in this policy are the Bank's own choice.

3. Definitions

  • Gross monthly income: basic salary plus fixed allowances and regular income from a defined source, verified by salary certificate, WPS credits, bank statements or UAEFTS consent. Variable pay is counted at the 12-month average after a 50% haircut. Self-declared income is never used alone.
  • DBR (debt burden ratio): total monthly obligations to all lenders ÷ gross monthly income. Credit cards and overdrafts count at 5% of the limit.
  • Salary-assigned loan: a loan where the employer confirms salary transfer to the Bank on the Bank's format and gives an irrevocable undertaking to pay end-of-service benefits to the Bank.
  • Unsecured (non-STL) loan: a loan where the salary is paid to another bank and repayment is collected by UAE Direct Debit (UAEDDS).
  • Employer category:
  • A: federal and local government, semi-government, Ministry of Defence, Armed Forces, Ministry of Interior.
  • B: listed large private employers.
  • C: other listed private employers.
  • D: non-listed employers.
  • Annex B gives the criteria.
  • AI grade: the AI Risk Score Card risk grade, 1 (lowest risk) to 10 (highest), with a 12-month probability of default.
  • Deviation: approval of a case that fails a Bank-policy rule (never a CBUAE rule), within Section 9.

4. Governance

  1. The Board Risk Committee approves this policy and its risk appetite.
  2. The Chief Risk Officer owns credit risk appetite. The Head of Retail Credit owns this policy and its parameters.
  3. Parameters are held as versioned configuration in the AI Risk Score Card. Any change needs maker-checker approval, and every decision is linked to the policy version in force.
  4. The policy is reviewed at least annually, after any CBUAE change, and when portfolio limits (Section 13) are breached. Internal Audit reviews compliance at least annually.

5.3 Affordability (DBR)

  • DBR ≤ 50% of gross monthly income after the new loan; ≤ 30% for retirees; government housing programmes up to 60%.
  • Home loans: DBR is assessed at the higher of the initial rate and the reversion rate (current 3-month EIBOR plus the reversion margin) P.
  • The Bank does not lend at the DBR maximum automatically. It considers essential expenses and dependants.
  • The AI Risk Score Card also reports a basic + housing DBR as a risk indicator. It is not a gate.

5.4 Verification and documents

  • All: Emirates ID, passport and residence visa (expats), AECB consent, signed application.
  • Salary-assigned: salary transfer letter on the Bank's format; 3 months' statements or UAEFTS consent; employer EOS undertaking.
  • Non-STL: salary certificate; 12 months' statements with regular salary credits; UAEDDS mandate.
  • Self-employed: trade licence, MOA, 12 months' personal and company statements; 2 years' audited financials for home loans.
  • Annex C lists documents by product.

5.5 Sector watch-list P

Credit Risk keeps a watch-list of employer sectors under stress. Watch-listed sectors get lower maximum amounts and AI-grade caps, not blanket exclusion.

6.3 Buyout, top-up and rescheduling

  • Buyout:
  • Security: salary transfer confirmation and EOS undertaking required.
  • Liability letter: no more than 15 days old.
  • Payment: the Bank pays the other bank directly within 30 days, and releases any remaining funds only after the clearance letter.
  • Limits: the combined loan stays within 20× income and 48 months.
  • Fee: no processing fee on a pure buyout.
  • Top-up:
  • Eligibility: only after ≥ 12 months of clean repayment (CBUAE).
  • Checks: fresh DBR and AECB check.
  • Frequency: at most one per 12 months.
  • Fee: processing fee on the increment only.
  • Rescheduling: under the collections policy. Beyond 48 months only when DBR exceeds 50% and with no new money.

7. Pricing and charges

  1. Pricing is risk-based by AI grade within the product floor and ceiling:
AI grade Probability of default (12 months) Personal loan add-on over floor
1–3 < 1% + 0.00–1.00%
4–6 1–5% + 1.00–5.00%
7–8 5–12% + 5.00–10.00% (referral only)
9–10 ≥ 12% Not offered
  1. All fees are within CBUAE caps and published in the tariff and a Key Facts Statement (Arabic and English, with APR) given before contract.
  2. Late fees are not compounded. No interest is charged on fees.
  3. VAT applies at the prevailing rate.

8. Credit decisioning and the AI Risk Score Card

  1. Each application is checked against three layers in order: CBUAE rules, this policy, and the credit parameters (Section 6).
  2. Policy always wins. A failed knock-out → Decline. A failed referral rule → Refer. All rules passed → Approve, unless the AI grade is 7 or worse, in which case the case is referred.
  3. The AI never approves a case that the policy rejects.
  4. The recommended amount is the lowest of all applicable caps, and the binding constraint is shown.
  5. Each decision is recorded with the policy version, model version, inputs and reasons, and can be replayed exactly.
  6. AI-drafted explanations and credit memos are drafts until a credit officer signs them.

10. Security, documentation and disbursement

  1. Security documents are executed and registered before disbursement: loan agreements, mortgage registration, RTA vehicle mortgage, deposit lien, gold pledge, employer letters.
  2. Insurance policies are assigned to the Bank before disbursement.
  3. Customers receive the KFS and contract in Arabic and English. The cooling-off period follows the CBUAE Consumer Protection Standards (verify current days).
  4. Buyout funds go directly to the other bank.

11. Monitoring, early warning and collections

  1. The AI Risk Score Card re-scores the whole book nightly. Watch-list triggers: - salary not credited, or a drop in salary credits - card utilisation above 85% - new arrears elsewhere (AECB) - spending above income (lifestyle mismatch)
  2. Early-warning cases are reviewed within 5 working days.
  3. Collections follow CBUAE rules on rescheduling (no new money beyond 48 months) and fair treatment.
  4. Classification and provisioning follow CBUAE C 3/2024 and IFRS 9: > 30 days past due → Stage 2; > 90 days past due → Stage 3.

12. Consumer protection

  • KFS before contract; APR disclosure; express consent for any limit increase; no automatic limit increases.
  • Complaints handled within CBUAE timelines.
  • Vulnerable customers receive tailored treatment and forbearance.
  • Arabic and English communications.

  • Any exception outside Section 9 needs Credit Committee approval and is reported to the Board Risk Committee quarterly.
