1. Purpose, scope and principles

1.1 Purpose

This policy sets the rules under which the Company lends to individuals in the UAE. It aims to build a small, high-quality retail book during the pilot years, comply fully with the Central Bank of the UAE (CBUAE) and the Finance Companies Regulation, and serve customers that banks reach poorly, at a fair price.

1.2 Scope

  • Customers: UAE nationals and UAE-resident expatriates, salaried, self-employed and retired. Non-residents are out of scope.
  • Phase 1 products:
    • Personal loans: employer-assigned and non-assigned, including buyout and top-up.
    • Credit cards.
    • Auto loans.
    • Gold-backed loans.
    • Salary advance (short-term credit).
  • Phase 2 products (each needs a CBUAE no-objection letter first):
    • BNPL (own short-term instalment credit).
    • Home finance, only if the licence covers mortgage finance.
  • Not offered: deposit-backed loans and overdrafts. The Company cannot take deposits from, or hold accounts for, individuals R.

1.3 Principles

  1. Regulation first. CBUAE and FCR rules are minimum standards. The Company may be stricter and is never more lenient.
  2. Responsible lending. Every customer's affordability is assessed, counting all debts including cards and BNPL. No customer is lent more than they can reasonably repay.
  3. Bank-grade selection, finance-company reach. The Company serves customers who keep their salary at a bank, earn modest incomes or are self-employed, using the same discipline a bank would.
  4. Capital-aware growth. Lending never outruns the capital, leverage and liquidity limits in Section 4.
  5. Fair treatment. No customer is excluded on nationality, gender or religion.
  6. AI recommends; people decide. The AI Risk Score Card provides grades, recommendations and explanations. Credit staff decide and are accountable (Section 10).

4. Prudential limits and funding R

4.1 Capital and leverage

  • Capital funds for every limit in this policy: AED 150m (paid-up capital at the regulatory minimum), updated quarterly from the financial statements.
  • Leverage: total liabilities ≤ 100% of capital funds in licence years 0–2. Higher multiples (200% after 2 years, then 300%, 500%, 700%) only with CBUAE approval.
  • Pilot book cap P: gross retail loans ≤ AED 250m in years 0–2. With AED 150m of capital and at most AED 150m of liabilities, total assets are capped at about AED 300m; the cap leaves room for the liquidity buffer and fixed assets.
  • Early warning P: when liabilities reach 85% of the leverage limit, Treasury and the Head of Credit slow new disbursements and report to the Board.

4.2 Large exposures and related parties

Limit Rule At AED 150m capital funds
Large exposure threshold ≥ 7% of capital funds AED 10.5m
Single borrower ≤ 10% of capital funds AED 15m
Connected group ≤ 15% of capital funds AED 22.5m
Principal shareholders; affiliates ≤ 10% single / 20% aggregate AED 15m / AED 30m
Board members No lending —

Every applicant is screened against the register of board members, principal shareholders, affiliates and their connected parties. Any related-party case goes to the Board Credit Committee.

4.3 Liquidity and funding

  • Liquid assets ≥ 10% of total liabilities (legal); internal trigger 12% P. No more than 25% of liquid assets in one bank.
  • Funding sources: capital, bank lines, deposits from legal entities (term ≥ 30 days; one depositor ≤ 20%), securitisation. Foreign funding ≤ 25% and hedged. Bonds need CBUAE approval.
  • No deposits or loans from individuals and no individual accounts. Therefore no deposit-backed lending and no overdrafts.
  • The monthly disbursement plan is agreed with Treasury so that lending never breaks the leverage or liquidity limits.

4.4 Pricing must cover the cost of risk P

A finance company pays more for funding than a bank and lends to riskier segments (sector net NPL 7.1% against 1.6% for banks). Each product's rate floor must cover, at the expected AI grade mix: funding cost, operating cost, expected loss and a return on capital. Illustrative build-up for a non-assigned personal loan: funding 6–7% + operating cost 4–5% + expected loss 3–7% + return on capital 2–3% ≈ 15–22%. Treasury and Finance confirm the build-up at each annual review.

5. Definitions

  • Gross monthly income: basic salary plus fixed allowances and regular income, verified by salary certificate, WPS credits, bank statements or UAEFTS consent. Variable pay counts at the 12-month average after a 50% haircut. Self-declared income is never used alone.
  • DBR (debt burden ratio): total monthly obligations to all lenders ÷ gross monthly income. Cards count at 5% of the limit; BNPL instalments count in full.
  • Employer-assigned loan A: a loan where the employer signs the Company's salary-assignment letter (salary paid to the customer's named bank account, not moved without the Company's clearance) and an irrevocable end-of-service (EOS) undertaking in favour of the Company, and repayment is collected by UAEDDS direct debit from that account. This replaces the bank "salary transfer", because the Company cannot hold salary accounts.
  • Non-assigned loan: a loan repaid by UAEDDS direct debit from the customer's salary account, with a security cheque, and no employer letter.
  • Employer category: A (government, semi-government, MoD, Armed Forces, MoI); B (listed large private employers); C (other listed private employers); D (non-listed). Annex B gives the criteria.
  • AI grade: the AI Risk Score Card grade, 1 (lowest risk) to 10 (highest), with a 12-month probability of default.
  • Capital funds: paid-up capital plus reserves, as defined by the FCR.
  • Deviation: approval of a case that fails a Company-policy rule (never a CBUAE or FCR rule), within Section 11.

6. Governance R

  1. The Board of Directors approves this policy, the risk appetite, the credit approval matrix (Section 11) and the creditworthiness procedures. The matrix and procedures are reviewed at least every 2 years; this policy at least annually.
  2. The Board Credit Committee (at least 3 members) oversees credit risk, approves exceptions and related-party cases, and receives the monthly portfolio report.
  3. The Chief Risk Officer owns risk appetite and portfolio limits. The Head of Credit owns this policy and its parameters.
  4. Parameters are held as versioned configuration in the AI Risk Score Card. Changes need maker-checker approval; every decision is linked to the policy version in force.
  5. Internal Audit reviews compliance at least annually.

7.3 Affordability (DBR)

  • DBR ≤ 50% of gross monthly income after the new credit; ≤ 30% past retirement age.
  • DBR counts all obligations in the AECB report and declared by the customer: loans, cards at 5% of the limit, BNPL instalments, guarantees given.
  • Rate-rise stress test for any variable-rate product, and a signed affordability summary in every file (Consumer Protection Standards).
  • The Company does not lend to the DBR maximum automatically. Essential expenses and dependants are considered, especially for incomes below AED 8,000.
  • No balloon payments or tenor stretching to pass the DBR test.

7.4 Verification and documents

  • All: Emirates ID, passport and residence visa (expats), AECB consent, signed application, KFS acknowledgement.
  • Employer-assigned: salary-assignment letter and EOS undertaking on the Company's format, signed by an authorised employer signatory; salary certificate; 3 months' statements or UAEFTS consent; UAEDDS mandate.
  • Non-assigned: salary certificate; 6 months' statements (12 months where available) with regular salary credits; UAEDDS mandate; security cheque.
  • Self-employed: trade licence, memorandum of association, 12 months' personal and company statements.
  • Annex C lists documents by product.

8.3 Buyout, top-up and rescheduling

  • Buyout:
    • Track: buyouts above AED 100,000 must be employer-assigned.
    • Liability letter: no more than 15 days old.
    • Payment: the Company pays the other lender directly; any remaining funds go to the customer only after the clearance letter.
    • Cost comparison: the customer receives and signs a written cost comparison of the old and new loans (Consumer Protection Standards). If the customer would pay less at their own bank by moving their salary, the comparison says so.
    • Limits: the combined loan stays within 20× income, 48 months and the product cap.
    • Fee: no processing fee on a pure buyout.
  • Top-up: only after ≥ 12 months of clean repayment; fresh DBR and AECB check; at most one per 12 months; processing fee on the increment only.
  • Rescheduling and deferral: under the collections policy. Beyond 48 months only when DBR exceeds 50% and with no new money. Up to 2 deferrals a year (fee AED 100 + VAT); rescheduling fee AED 250 + VAT.

9.3 Rules

  1. All fees are within CBUAE and FCR caps and published in the tariff and a Key Facts Statement (Arabic and English, with APR) given before the contract.

  3. VAT applies at the prevailing rate.

10. Credit decisioning and the AI Risk Score Card

  1. Each application is checked against three layers in order: regulatory rules (CBUAE and FCR), this policy, and the product parameters (Section 8).
  2. Policy always wins. A failed knock-out → Decline. A failed referral rule → Refer. All rules passed → Approve, unless the AI grade is 7 or worse, in which case the case is referred.
  3. The AI never approves a case that the policy rejects.
  4. The recommended amount is the lowest of all applicable caps (income multiple, product cap, DBR headroom, internal per-individual cap, remaining single-borrower headroom), and the binding constraint is shown.
  5. Before each approval the system checks the prudential limits (Section 4) and the portfolio limits (Section 15).
  6. Each decision is recorded with the policy version, model version, inputs and reasons, and can be replayed exactly.
  7. AI-drafted explanations and credit memos are drafts until a credit officer signs them.
  8. The AI grades are validated on finance-company data before launch and monitored monthly; until then, referral thresholds are applied conservatively.

12. Security, documentation and disbursement

  1. Security documents are executed and registered before disbursement: loan agreements, employer assignment letters and EOS undertakings, UAEDDS mandates, RTA vehicle mortgages, gold pledge and custody receipts.
  2. Security cheques: dated, for no more than 120% of the loan, never blank, stored under dual control and returned on settlement.
  3. Insurance policies are assigned to the Company before disbursement.
  4. Customers receive the KFS and contract in Arabic and English. The 5-business-day cooling-off period applies; the customer may waive it only in writing.
  5. Approved loans are disbursed within 10 business days of the signed contract.
  6. Buyout funds go directly to the other lender.

13. Consumer protection and sales conduct

  • KFS before contract; APR disclosure; express consent for any limit increase; no automatic limit increases.
  • Telemarketing: explicit prior consent; calls only 9am–6pm; agents trained for at least 15 hours; call records kept.
  • Agents and partners (distribution agents, BNPL merchants) follow this policy and are audited at least annually.
  • Consolidation and buyouts: a written cost comparison signed by the customer.
  • Complaints handled within CBUAE timelines.
  • Vulnerable customers (including low-income workers) receive plain-language explanations, tailored treatment and forbearance.
  • Arabic and English communications.

14. Monitoring, early warning, collections and provisioning

  1. The AI Risk Score Card re-scores the whole book nightly. Watch-list triggers:
    • salary not credited, or a change of salary account on an employer-assigned loan
    • card utilisation above 85%
    • new arrears or new BNPL plans elsewhere (AECB)
    • repeated salary advances (4 or more in 6 months)
    • returned direct debits
  2. Early-warning cases are reviewed within 5 working days.
  3. Collections follow CBUAE rules on rescheduling (no new money beyond 48 months), deferral limits and fair treatment. No harassment; contact hours as for telemarketing.
  4. Classification and provisioning follow CBUAE C 3/2024 and IFRS 9: > 30 days past due → Stage 2; > 90 days past due → Stage 3 (default). Write-off no later than 180 days past due until the CBUAE maximum is confirmed R.

16. Exceptions and review

  • Any exception outside Section 11 needs Board Credit Committee approval and is reported to the Board quarterly.
  • This policy is reviewed at least annually; the credit approval matrix and creditworthiness procedures at least every 2 years. Benchmarks are refreshed against UAE finance companies, digital and BNPL lenders and the top five banks at each review.
  • After 12 months of pilot data, management reviews the A and P items, especially the AECB floors, the AED 7,000 non-assigned minimum and the lower-income segment (AED 3,000–4,999).

A Federal and local government, semi-government, MoD, Armed Forces, MoI Best pricing
B Listed private employers: ≥ 3 years trading, ≥ 100 UAE staff on WPS, satisfactory financials Standard pricing
C Other listed private employers: ≥ 2 years trading, ≥ 25 UAE staff on WPS Standard caps
D Non-listed employers Employer-assigned max AED 150,000; non-assigned needs AED 7,000 income; portfolio limit 25%

Employers sign a framework agreement before their staff can use the employer-assigned track.
