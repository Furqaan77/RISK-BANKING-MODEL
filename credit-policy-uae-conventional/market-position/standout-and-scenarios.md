# How Our Bank Credit Policy Stands Out in the UAE Market

Applies to the conventional bank policy. The Islamic edition shares the same credit criteria.

All customers are illustrative. Market figures come from the banks' own published terms (checked 9 Nov 2026). Calculations use the policy's rates and rules.

## The six things that make us different
1. **We say "yes" where others say "no", safely.**
   - **Without salary transfer:** most top banks want AED 8,000–10,000 (FAB Islamic 15,000). We accept **AED 5,000** when the customer has a strong credit score (AECB ≥ 600), a listed employer and 6 months in the job.
   - **Auto loans:** most top banks want AED 7,000. We accept **AED 5,000**.
   - **First home for UAE nationals:** Mashreq and RAKBANK want AED 15,000. We accept **AED 10,000**, after a stress test.
2. **Products the big conventional banks don't offer:** salary advance, finance secured by end-of-service benefits, and lending against gold. We also publish a clear rule for credit card limits.
3. **Fair, transparent pricing:**
   - **Rates:** close to the market middle, priced by risk grade, so good customers pay less.
   - **Buyouts:** no processing fee.
   - **Home loans:** 25% free prepayment a year, a lower valuation fee, and processing capped at AED 50,000.
4. **Speed:** the AI Risk Score Card decides in **seconds**. Buyouts are paid to the other bank **within 30 days**, and nationals get up to **120 days** before the first instalment.
5. **Safety built in:**
   - **Credit-score floors:** minimum AECB scores, with automatic declines for serious arrears.
   - **Home-loan stress test:** home loans are tested at a higher rate.
   - **Nightly early warning:** every customer is re-checked each night.
   - **Salary diversion:** the rate rises by 2% if salary stops coming in.
   - **Portfolio limits:** we grow, but within limits.
6. **Fair to everyone:** no nationality bans. Employers are grouped by risk, not excluded.

## Realistic scenarios

### Scenario 1: A nurse whose salary goes to another bank
**Anita, 29, expat nurse at a listed private hospital (category C).** Salary AED 6,500, paid to another bank. AECB score 655, 14 months in the job, a credit card with AED 10,000 limit.

| Lender | Answer |
|---|---|
| FAB (non-listed / no STL) | Needs AED 10,000 → **no** |
| Emirates NBD (no STL, per comparison site) | Needs AED 10,000 → **no** |
| RAKBANK (no STL) | Yes: AECB ≥ 541; up to 10× salary at **12.99–22.49%** |
| Mashreq NEO | Yes: up to AED 150,000 at 10.49–17.49% |
| **Our policy** | **Yes.** AECB 655 ≥ 600; listed employer; 14 months > 6 |

**Our offer:**
- **Limit:** the lower of 10× salary (AED 65,000) and the AED 200,000 cap, so **AED 65,000**.
- **Affordability:** 50% of AED 6,500 = AED 3,250, minus AED 500 for the card = **AED 2,750** of room each month.
- **Repayment:** at grade 4 (11.99%) over 48 months, the instalment is about **AED 1,711**, within the room. At RAKBANK's lowest rate (12.99%) it would be about AED 1,743.

### Scenario 2: Moving a loan to us (buyout), UAE national
**Khalid, 34, UAE national, Abu Dhabi government employee (category A).** Salary AED 32,000. His existing loan at another bank has **AED 400,000** outstanding at **8.49%**, with 36 months left.

- **Our offer:** we pay the other bank directly within 30 days and charge **no processing fee**. His rate becomes **5.49%** (grade 1–3).
- **Savings:**
  - The instalment drops from about **AED 12,625** to **AED 12,077** a month, saving **AED 549 a month** or about **AED 19,750 over 36 months**.
  - The other bank's early-settlement charge is 1%, or AED 4,000, so the net saving is about **AED 15,750**.
- **Extra cash:** he can add AED 100,000 at the same time. The total of AED 500,000 is within his limit of 20× salary (AED 640,000), and over 48 months the instalment of about AED 11,626 is **36% of salary**, under the 50% DBR limit.

### Scenario 3: First car for a lower-paid worker
**Joseph, 31, expat supervisor at a logistics company.** Salary AED 5,500 transferred to us. He wants a **used car for AED 60,000** (4 years old).

| Lender | Answer |
|---|---|
| Emirates NBD, FAB, Mashreq | Need AED 7,000 → **no** |
| ADCB, RAKBANK | AED 5,000 → yes |
| **Our policy** | **Yes**, minimum AED 5,000 |

**Our offer:**
- **Loan:** AED 12,000 down payment (20%), loan of **AED 48,000** at 3.19% flat over 60 months.
- **Repayment:** interest AED 7,656, so the instalment is about **AED 928 a month**, or **17% of salary**.
- **Car age:** 4 years now + 5 years of loan = 9 years at the end, within the 10-year limit.

### Scenario 4: First home for a young Emirati
**Mariam, 28, UAE national, semi-government employer (category A).** Salary AED 12,000. First home in Sharjah, AED 900,000.

| Lender | Answer |
|---|---|
| Mashreq, RAKBANK | Need AED 15,000 → **no** |
| Emirates NBD (10,000), ADCB (8,000) | Yes |
| **Our policy** | **Yes**, minimum AED 10,000 for nationals |

**Our offer:**
- **Loan:** 85% of value = **AED 765,000** over 25 years.
- **Repayment at our 3-year fixed rate (4.24%):** about **AED 4,140 a month**.
- **Stress test:** at the reversion rate (3-month EIBOR 4.39% + 1.79% = 6.18%), about **AED 5,013 a month** = **42% of salary**, still under 50%. She passes the stress test, not just today's rate.

### Scenario 5: Using end-of-service benefits to get a better rate
**Suresh, 45, expat engineer at a listed large company (category B), 9 years' service.** Basic salary AED 14,000, gross AED 22,000. He needs **AED 100,000** for his child's university.

- **His end-of-service benefit:** 21 days' basic for each of the first 5 years plus 30 days for each of the next 4 = 225 days, or about **AED 103,562**. 80% of that = about **AED 82,849**.
- **Security:** with his employer's undertaking, the benefit covers **83%** of the loan. That lowers the bank's risk, so he can be priced near the floor.
- **Saving:** **5.99%** instead of 9.99% means about **AED 2,348 a month** instead of AED 2,536, saving about **AED 9,000** over 48 months.
- **Market:** no big UAE bank lends against end-of-service benefits. They only take them as extra security.

### Scenario 6: Help before payday
**Ramesh, 40, construction foreman.** Net salary AED 5,200 transferred to us. He needs **AED 2,500** for school fees 10 days before payday.

- **Our salary advance:** up to 50% of net salary (AED 2,600). AED 2,500 is approved for a fixed fee of **AED 100**, repaid automatically from the next salary.
- **Market:** none of the five largest conventional banks publishes a salary advance. Only the Islamic banks DIB and ADIB offer one.

### Scenario 7: Borrowing against gold
**Fatima, 50, with gold certificates of 200 g (worth about AED 90,000 at an illustrative AED 450/g).**

- **Our offer:** 70% = about **AED 63,000**.
- **Safety:** if gold falls **12.5%**, we ask her to top up. If it falls **17.6%**, we sell the gold after notice.
- **Market:** no conventional UAE bank offers this. Only Emirates Islamic lends against gold, at 50%, and only to Priority customers.

### Scenario 8: Where we say "no", and why that is good risk management
**Same profile as Anita, but with an AECB score of 560 and a non-listed employer (category D).**

- **Without salary transfer:** she needs **AED 10,000** and a score of 600 → **declined**.
- **Our counter-offer:** move her salary to us. At AED 5,000 and a score ≥ 541, the salary-assigned loan is available. The customer is not just turned away.
- **Four months later:** if her salary stops arriving, the **nightly check** flags it. The rate steps up by 2% and the collections team calls her **within 5 working days**, before a missed payment becomes a default.
