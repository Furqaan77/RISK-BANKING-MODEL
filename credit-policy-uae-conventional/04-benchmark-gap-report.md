Prepared 2026-11-09 for the bank's management and credit team. It tests the recommended retail credit criteria (carried over unchanged from the Islamic policy work and converted to conventional structures) against the published criteria of the **top five conventional UAE retail banks**. ## 4. Lenient-edge items: tighten or keep?

### P-31: Home loan minimum salary for UAE nationals: AED 10,000

- **What the five do:** ADCB 8k; ENBD 10k; Mashreq 15k; RAKBANK 15k; FAB not published (15k per aggregator). Expats: 15k at all five, which matches the policy.
- **Risk:** A 25-year mortgage has to survive rate resets. At AED 10,000 the customer has a thinner buffer for service charges, maintenance and insurance than at the 15k used by Mashreq and RAKBANK.
- **Option:** Keep AED 10,000 (in line with ENBD), or tighten to AED 12,500-15,000 for nationals outside government schemes. Keep AED 8,000 under SZHP / MBRHE / ADHA top-up.
- **Trade-off:** Tightening removes some lower-income national borrowers outside the schemes; the government-scheme channel keeps access for them.
- **Worked example:** At 50% DBR and a ~6% stressed rate over 25 years: AED 10,000 salary funds about AED 776,034; AED 12,500 about AED 970,043; AED 15,000 about AED 1,164,051.

### P-39: Home loan service for salaried expats: 6 months + probation

- **What the five do:** ENBD requires expats to have ≥ 3 years in the UAE and ≥ 1 year in the current job. The other four do not publish a figure.
- **Risk:** Recently arrived expats have shorter local credit history and higher job and residence mobility, which raises early-default and repatriation risk on long mortgages.
- **Option:** Option to tighten: 12 months in the current job and ≥ 3 years' UAE residence (ENBD standard), or keep 6 months but cap LTV at 70% for expats with < 2 years in the UAE.
- **Trade-off:** Tightening excludes new arrivals who can afford a home; the LTV-cap alternative keeps them eligible with more equity at risk.
- **Worked example:** An expat earning AED 25,000 who arrived 8 months ago passes the current policy at 80% LTV; under the ENBD-style rule they would wait until month 36, or (alternative) borrow at 70% LTV.

### P-43: Auto loan minimum salary: AED 5,000

- **What the five do:** ADCB and RAKBANK accept AED 5,000; ENBD, FAB and Mashreq require AED 7,000 (Mashreq also requires salary transfer).
- **Risk:** Lower-income car buyers are more exposed to insurance, fuel and maintenance costs on top of the instalment; repossession recoveries on used cars are uncertain.
- **Option:** Keep AED 5,000 (ADCB, RAKBANK level) with salary transfer mandatory below AED 7,000 and a maximum of 10x monthly salary; or tighten to the AED 7,000 median.
- **Trade-off:** Tightening loses the 5-7k segment that two of the five still serve; the STL-plus-cap option keeps them with a stronger repayment source.
- **Worked example:** At AED 5,000 with no other debts, 50% DBR leaves AED 2,500 a month; a AED 50,000 new-car loan at 2.49% flat over 60 months costs about AED 937 a month.

### P-49: Self-employed auto loans: average balance AED 15,000

- **What the five do:** ENBD requires an average balance of AED 20,000; FAB AED 25,000; RAKBANK ≥ 1 year in business; ADCB eligible without a published figure.
- **Risk:** Self-employed income is volatile; the average balance is the main buffer when business receipts fall.
- **Option:** Tighten to AED 20,000 (ENBD level), or keep 15,000 with a 6-month bank-statement turnover test (credits ≥ 3x the instalment).
- **Trade-off:** Tightening excludes smaller traders; the turnover test keeps them where cash flow is proven.
- **Worked example:** A AED 100,000 loan at 2.49% flat over 60 months costs about AED 1,874 a month: AED 15,000 covers about 8.0 months of instalments, AED 20,000 about 10.7 months.

**Data caveats**

- Emirates NBD's conventional retail book is an approximation: group retail & wealth gross loans (AED 184bn) less Emirates Islamic's retail segment assets (~AED 53bn, which include some SME). It is still clearly the largest.
- FAB's retail book (~AED 94bn) was read from chart labels in the investor presentation (M) and includes international books and the small FAB Islamic window.
- ADCB personal-loan and card figures are search-engine extracts (M) because the bank's site blocked automated reading; one ADCB FAQ is about 4 years old. ADCB's book includes ADCB Islamic.
- Commercial Bank of Dubai is not ranked: it does not disclose a retail split (AED 105bn gross loans, corporate-led).
- ENBD's AED 10,000 non-STL minimum salary and FAB's home-loan minimum salary (15k) come from comparison sites (L). Mashreq's own pages show AED 8,000 and AED 10,000 for unapproved employers.
- Card limit multiples, most AECB floors and home-loan stress rates are not published by any of the five; the policy values there are proposals.
- Several mortgage rate grids come from broker market briefs (L) and move with EIBOR (3M EIBOR 4.39% on 1-Oct-26; CBUAE base rate 3.90% from 17-Sep-26).
- All figures were read on 9 Nov 2026 from the research in ../credit-policy-uae/01-benchmark-unsecured.md and 02-benchmark-secured.md (conventional products only).
