**Problem:** Banks make credit decisions on scattered, hard-to-verify information. Staff cannot quickly find reliable, citable answers to risk and compliance questions (which guidance applies, is it current, which authoritative source). Credit risk assessment is also slow and inconsistent across **individuals, businesses and corporates**. Result: delays, inconsistent interpretations, repeated escalations to risk and compliance teams, operational losses, defaults and regulatory penalties.

**What we build:** a secure, AI-powered Credit Risk Assessment Platform that runs inside each bank's private infrastructure (customer data stays under bank control). It:
1. Assigns risk grades 1–10 to individuals, businesses and corporates, combining internal bank data with regularly updated external data (credit bureau, business credit, market, sanctions, adverse media) through secure, controlled, approved APIs.
2. Enforces central bank rules, bank policy and credit policy through a hierarchical policy engine. Policy overrides the AI model. Higher-risk cases go to a human.
3. Lets staff ask risk and compliance questions in plain English and get a clear answer in seconds, with verifiable citations to current sources, flagging uncertainty and the need for expert review. (Treated as a third capability; the user may later say it is a separate product.)

Two editions: UAE Islamic and Global (US and UK).

**Success:** faster, more consistent, compliant decisions; fewer avoidable mistakes; less routine work for risk and compliance teams; lower operating costs, fewer fines and losses, fewer defaults, better recoveries.

**Why:** the user pasted two statements and asked for one merged statement on 2026-11-11, then told me to treat it as the problem we must solve.

**How to apply:** frame plans, prompts, expert-interview questions and documents around this. Scope covers individuals, businesses AND corporates, not only retail. The external data is fetched through approved APIs, even though the model runs on-prem. The retail credit policy packs are example policies the engine would run on, not the whole product.
