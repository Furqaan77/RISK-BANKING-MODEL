# Digest corp-rules-r1-1: Corporate lending "hard limits" rulebook (UAE, UK, US)

- Research date: 2026-11-13 (all sources accessed 2026-11-13)
- Scope: hard limits a bank must respect when lending to large companies (corporates)
- Status: complete for round 1 (UAE, UK, US). 34 claims, about 20 distinct sources.
- Method: primary sources first. The CBUAE rulebook (rulebook.centralbank.ae) blocks the fetch tool but was read in full through a plain HTTP download. Every figure below was read from the source text, not from memory. Where a figure could not be confirmed, it sits in Part D.
- Capital base used in each country is stated in each row. Do not mix them (UAE = Tier 1; UK = Tier 1; US national banks = Tier 1 + Tier 2 + ALLL "capital and surplus"; US SCCL = Tier 1).

## Part C: Conflicts and leads

1. **KPMG vs the CBUAE text on "default" triggers.** KPMG (May 2025) says that under C 3/2024 a downgrade of 2 or more notches, or a PD increase of 100% or more, is treated as default. The Standards text on the rulebook does not contain these triggers. It lists an external downgrade only as one SICR (Stage 2) indicator. These triggers may come from separate CBUAE guidance. Do not code them as default triggers until that guidance is checked.
2. **The capital base differs by country.** The UAE and the UK use Tier 1 capital. The US Part 32 limit uses "capital and surplus" (Tier 1 + Tier 2 + excess allowance), which is a larger base. Applying "25%" to US banks would be wrong: the US rule is 15% + 10%. The US SCCL (25% of Tier 1) applies only to the largest holding companies.
3. **US interbank.** Part 32 has no interbank exemption, but overnight fed funds are outside the "loan" definition. Reg F adds a separate 25%-of-total-capital cap, only for correspondents not shown to be adequately capitalized.
4. **UAE real-estate cap.** Many market summaries still quote "20% of deposits". Press from 2018 to 2021 says this fixed cap was replaced by a flexible, RWA-based supervisory framework. The rulebook text of the 2021 real-estate standard was not retrieved, and no numeric threshold was confirmed. Lead: search rulebook.centralbank.ae for the 2021 real-estate exposure notice (**verify**).
5. **US leveraged lending is split by regulator.** OCC and FDIC banks: the 2013 guidance is gone. Fed-supervised state member banks and BHCs: SR 13-3 is still listed and not marked superseded, and law-firm memos note an open question on its legal effect. Treat "6x" as a historical benchmark, not a rule.
6. **UK output floor in 2030.** The rule text gives transitional factors for 2027 to 2029 only. The 72.5% steady state from 2030 is inferred from Art. 92(2A). Older 2024-25 commentary agrees.
7. **UK large exposure reporting frequency.** The extract said "at least semi-annual" for certain reports. COREP large-exposure reporting is commonly quarterly. Confirm before using a frequency.
8. **UAE grandfathering ends 31 Dec 2026.** From that date, every UAE bank must meet the 25% and related-party limits in full. This is a live 2026-2027 change worth flagging in the rulebook config.
9. **US Basel III endgame.** The March 2026 re-proposal is not final. In 2026 the Fed finalized other items (CBLR change on 23 Apr 2026; stress-test averaging on 30 Sep 2026), but not the credit-risk rewrite. Keep US capital parameters on the current 12 CFR 217.
10. **Provision floor is UAE-only.** The UAE has a hard 1.50%-of-CRWA floor (and no UAE IRB regime was found). The UK and US have IRB options but no regulatory provision floor. A multi-country engine should hold "provision floor" as a UAE-only parameter.
