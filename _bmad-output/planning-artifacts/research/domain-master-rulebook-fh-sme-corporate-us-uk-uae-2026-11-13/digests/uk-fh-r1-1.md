# UK finance houses (non-bank consumer lenders): rules and four lenders. Digest R1-1

Researched: 2026-11-13. Scope: UK non-bank consumer credit firms ("finance houses"). Status: COMPLETE for round 1. About 20 distinct sources (FCA, legislation.gov.uk, UK Supreme Court, Bank of England, SEC 8-K, company reports and product pages). Gaps are in Part D.

Key 2026 facts for the rulebook: (1) HCSTC cap unchanged after the FCA's 2026 review (Sept 2026: no stage 2). (2) Motor finance redress scheme PS26/3 is partly SUSPENDED by the Upper Tribunal since 2 Jul 2026; hearing Dec 2026 or Feb 2027. (3) Non-bank lenders have no fixed capital ratio; only "appropriate resources" (COND 2.4).

Plain-language note for new risk staff: a "finance house" is a lender that is not a bank. It lends its own (or borrowed) money but cannot take savings deposits. In the UK it needs an FCA "consumer credit" permission. Most of the conduct rules are the same as for banks. The big differences are capital (non-banks have almost no capital rule) and deposits (non-banks cannot take them).

Notes on Part B (plain language):
- **n/p** = not published in the source I could open.
- **Vanquis and Close Brothers are banks**, not finance houses. They hold deposit-taking permission (PRA + FCA). They are included as benchmarks because they compete with finance houses. H&T and Novuna are non-bank (no retail deposits) as far as found.
- **H&T impairment** is not a normal "bad debt" rate. Pawn losses are low because the gold/watch pledge usually covers the loan (LTV c.65%). The GBP 29.3m "impairment" mostly = uncollected interest on forfeited loans, recovered by selling the item.
- **Motor finance redress provisions** (not credit losses): Close Brothers c.GBP 300m at 31-Jan-2026 (secondary sources: c.GBP 320m by FY2026); Mitsubishi HC Capital UK GBP 61.0m total (GBP 56.0m charged FY2026; exceptional item GBP 47.8m in Consumer Finance segment).
