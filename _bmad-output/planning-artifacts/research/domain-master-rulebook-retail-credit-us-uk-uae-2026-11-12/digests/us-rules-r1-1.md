# US federal rulebook for retail (consumer) credit: research digest r1-1

- Scope: hard limits and mandatory requirements a US bank must follow when lending to individuals (federal level only).
- Accessed: 2026-11-12. Status: COMPLETE (round 1). Open items are in Parts C and D.
- Method: regulation text pulled directly from the eCFR API (Title 12 "up to date as of 2026-11-09", latest amendment 2026-11-04; Title 32 up to date as of 2026-11-09). Other items from CFPB, Fed, OCC, FDIC, FHFA, Fannie Mae pages.
- Plain-language note: "Reg Z" = Truth in Lending rules (12 CFR 1026). "Reg B" = Equal Credit Opportunity rules (12 CFR 1002). "QM" = Qualified Mortgage, a "safe" mortgage type that gets legal protection. "APOR" = Average Prime Offer Rate, a market benchmark rate published weekly.

## Part C: Leads and contradictions

- C1. CONTRADICTION, late fees: eCFR (2026-11-09) still prints the 2024 "$8" safe harbor in 1026.52(b)(1)(ii), but the rule was vacated on 15 Apr 2025. The CFPB's 2026 annual-adjustment rule (2025-22773) sets no penalty-fee amounts, and no Federal Register document restoring the old text was found. So the safe-harbor dollar amount in force today is unclear (the pre-2024 indexed figures were around $30 first / $41 repeat, from memory, NOT verified). Action: read the court's final judgment (Chamber of Commerce v. CFPB, N.D. Tex.; case name and docket number from memory, verify) and ask counsel. For the model: treat late fees as "lender sets, must be reasonable and proportional", not a fixed number.
- C2. CONTRADICTION, AI adverse-action guidance: Circulars 2022-03 and 2023-03 are withdrawn (May 2025), yet many vendor and law-firm pages still quote them as current. The binding rule (Reg B 1002.9 "specific reasons", FCRA 1681m key factors) is unchanged. Design implication: the scorecard must still produce specific, accurate reason codes per declined applicant.
- C3. CHANGE, disparate impact: the Reg B final rule (effective 21 Jul 2026) says ECOA does not support disparate-impact liability. But (a) the Fair Housing Act and state laws (e.g., some state AI and fair-lending laws) may still apply disparate-impact tests; (b) the rule may be challenged in court; (c) a future administration can reverse it. Recommend keeping disparate-impact testing as good practice. Not verified in this pass: any lawsuit against 91 FR 21620.
- C4. CHANGE, model risk: SR 11-7 is no longer current. Secondary sites still cite SR 11-7 as the standard. Also: SR 26-2 says it is most relevant to banks over $30bn and is non-enforceable, but OCC Bulletin 2026-13 says it "applies to all community banks, subject to its stated limitations" (as summarised by the fetch tool). Read the OCC bulletin text directly before relying on either reading. The agencies were reported (secondary sources only) to plan a separate request for information on AI model risk: not verified.
- C5. LEAD, mortgage credit: CFPB Request for Information "Promoting Access to Mortgage Credit" (91 FR 42382, 9 Jul 2026) may lead to changes to ATR/QM (e.g., price thresholds or seasoning). Watch item; not read in full.
- C6. LEAD, FHA: ML 2026-11 changes the FHA Minimum Decision Credit Score method for alternative scores (e.g., VantageScore), effective 1 Jan 2027. Credit-score inputs to the model may need to accept more than one score type.
- C7. LEAD, conforming loan limit: FHFA normally announces the next year's limits in late November; the 2027 figure is likely to be published within weeks of this digest.
- C8. LEAD, charge-off policy: the FFIEC Uniform Retail Credit Classification policy dates from 2000 and talks about "incurred" losses (pre-CECL). No rescission was found, but no 2025-26 reaffirmation was found either. Check the current OCC Comptroller's Handbook (Retail Lending / Credit Card Lending booklets) and FDIC RMS Manual.
- C9. LEAD, BNPL: federal gap; New York's Buy Now Pay Later Act (2025) and NYDFS proposed rule reported by secondary sources. Out of scope for the federal rulebook but relevant for state overlays.
- C10. Applicability note: CFPB Reg B and Reg Z apply to all banks, but the CFPB supervises only banks over $10bn; OCC/FDIC/Fed examine smaller banks for the same rules. SR 26-2 is guidance, not regulation. Fannie/FHA/VA DTI limits bind only loans sold to or insured by those programs.
